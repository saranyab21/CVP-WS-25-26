Q5.2.1
Your implementation is a simplified version of Uijlings et al. (Selective Search). In what ways does their version differ from yours?

1. One run vs many: I run SS once; the paper runs it many times with different settings/color spaces and unions all boxes.

2. Simpler cues: I use HSV + LBP; they use multiple color spaces and richer texture filters.

3. Neighbor check: I approximate neighbors via bbox overlap; they use true pixel adjacency.

4. Cleanup/ranking: I mostly keep all regions; they deduplicate/rank and vary cue weights for diversity.

5. Model used: I use a pretrained CNN + SVM; classic pipelines often used SIFT/HOG (not SS itself).

Q5.2.2
What is the effect of changing the thresholds for positive and negative samples? Why do we need two thresholds instead of just one?

Changing the thresholds for positive (tp) and negative (tn) samples affects how strict the labeling is for SVM training. A higher positive threshold 
(e.g., 0.8) means only proposals that very closely match the ground truth are labeled as positive, which gives fewer but higher-quality positives. 
A lower negative threshold (e.g., 0.2) means only proposals far from any object are labeled negative. The region between the two thresholds (ambiguous cases) 
is ignored to avoid confusing the SVM with hard samples. If I used only one threshold, I would have to label many ambiguous regions as either positive or 
negative, which would likely hurt the classifier's performance. Using two thresholds gives cleaner training data and a more robust detector.

Q5.2.3
The balloon dataset is quite small and does not provide many training samples. Can you think of ways to increase the amount of training data?

Yes - if I had more time or resources, I would try data augmentation (random crops, flips, rotations, color jitter, etc.) to multiply the amount of positive 
regions. I could also generate synthetic positive samples by jittering ground truth boxes or using copy-paste augmentation. Using balloon-like images from 
the web or related datasets for pre-training or as extra positives would help as well. Finally, even just using all ground truth boxes (and slightly 
perturbed versions) for positives—as suggested in the assignment—would help increase the number of training samples for SVM.
